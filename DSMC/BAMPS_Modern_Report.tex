\section{Executive Summary}
This report details the completion and validation of the modern BAMPS GPU engine. The architecture utilizes a high-performance, template-based policy framework supporting Classical, Relativistic, and Ultrarelativistic regimes. We have established rigorous physical credibility by benchmarking against standard Lennard-Jones (LJ) fluids and Argon structures, with direct comparisons against LAMMPS reference data.

\section{Architecture Design}
The engine utilizes a C++ template-based policy architecture:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Thermostats}: Includes \texttt{Andersen}, \texttt{Langevin}, and a true \texttt{Nosé-Hoover Chain (NHC)}.
    \item \textbf{NHC Implementation}: Implemented as a global operator using a GPU kinetic energy reduction and Host-side state propagation (MTK algorithm/Yoshida-Suzuki integration). This ensures rigorous canonical sampling.
    \item \textbf{Potentials}: \texttt{LennardJones} with $2.5\sigma$ cutoff and force capping. Current validation is performed in pure MD mode (\texttt{NullCollision}) to establish a structural baseline.
\end{itemize}

\section{Physical Validation: Standard RDF Control}
To prove the numerical accuracy of our force calculation and thermostat implementation, we performed a benchmark on liquid Argon ($\rho^*=0.8, T^*=1.0$) and compared it against an independent \textbf{LAMMPS Reference}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RDF Comparison}: Figure~\ref{fig:argon} (right) shows the Radial Distribution Function $g(r)$. The BAMPS GPU engine (red line) perfectly overlaps with the standard LAMMPS reference (black dashed line), capturing the exact peak positions and shell structures.
    \item \textbf{Conservation}: NVE simulations (Figure~\ref{fig:argon}, left) show negligible energy drift, confirming the stability of our Velocity Verlet integration.
\end{itemize}
